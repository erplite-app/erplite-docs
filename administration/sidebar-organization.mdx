---
title: Sidebar Organization
description: "Organize tables and pages into folders for easier navigation."
---

Administrators can organize the sidebar so users can find the tables and work areas they need. This is useful when a workspace has many tables across sales, procurement, production, dispatch, finance, or support operations.

## What Sidebar Folders Do

Sidebar folders group navigation items without changing the underlying table or record data. A folder can contain related tables such as:

- Procurement: Purchase Orders, GRNs, Vendors, Vendor Items.
- Sales: Sales Orders, Customers, Dispatches.
- Production: Production Orders, BoMs, Inventory Ledger.
- Admin: Users, settings, configuration tables.

## Manage Folders

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open sidebar settings">
    Go to the sidebar management area from Settings or the configured navigation control in your workspace.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create folders">
    Add folders that match how users think about their work.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Drag items into folders">
    Move tables or links into the right folder. You can also drag items out if they should be shown at the top level.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save">
    Save the folder layout so it is available to users.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Best Practices

- Keep folder names short and operational.
- Put the most frequently used tables near the top.
- Avoid hiding critical work queues deep inside several folders.
- Review the sidebar after adding new tables or reports.

## AI Guidance

### When to Use This Feature

An AI should organize the sidebar when users have too many tables to scan quickly or when a rollout adds a new business module.

### How to Use It

1. Group tables by business area.
2. Put daily work queues first.
3. Keep admin/configuration tables separate from operating tables.
4. Save and test with target users.

### How to Verify

The AI should verify that users can find their most important tables without search and that restricted tables remain hidden from users without access.
