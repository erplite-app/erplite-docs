## The three policies

A **claim** records that units of a grain — item × warehouse — are spoken for by a dispatch line. What that claim *does* depends entirely on the account's reservation policy.

| Policy | Allocation | A competing allocation | Available |
|---|---|---|---|
| `none` | Takes no claim | Succeeds. The second **shipment** is refused by the negative-stock floor at posting | **Yes**, the default |
| `advisory` | Records a claim | Succeeds, with a shortfall warning naming the competing claim | **Yes** |
| `enforced` | Holds the stock in Inventory | **Refused**, stating available, requested and shortfall | **No** |

<Warning>
**`enforced` cannot be selected.** The policy write refuses it and names why: it needs the Inventory reservation operation, the posting path refusing anything that would drive on-hand below reserved, and the commitments-tracked flag. Until all three exist, `enforced` would refuse a competing claim and nothing else — which is what `advisory` already gives.

The refusal writes nothing. It does **not** silently downgrade you to `advisory`.
</Warning>

Under `advisory`, every screen must label the number as advisory. The stock is not actually held, and presenting it as though it were is the failure the labelling exists to prevent.

## Reading the policy

```http
GET /reservation-policies      → config.manage
PUT /reservation-policies      → config.manage
```

A policy has a **scope**: `account`, `warehouse` or `material_role`. One scope holds one policy.

```json
{
  "scope_type": "warehouse",
  "scope_ref": "WH-MAIN",
  "policy_name": "advisory",
  "params": {},
  "reason": "Switching the main warehouse to advisory ahead of the pilot."
}
```

A reason is **mandatory** and checked before anything else, so a nonsense request with no reason is refused for the reason, not for the nonsense.

If two active policies somehow exist at one scope, the write refuses and **names both rows** rather than picking one — the platform's uniqueness is a search-then-write that can lose a race, so that state is reachable.

### When no policy exists

The resolver falls back to `none` and says so. Allocation and shipping continue, new allocations take no claim, and **existing claims are still held and still shown**. The account is labelled as having no active reservation policy. Nothing is blocked, and no screen calls the stock unreserved while a claim exists.

## Reading claims

```http
GET /claims        → view
```

Filter by `item` and `warehouse`.

```json
{
  "grains": [
    {
      "item_code": "SKU-001",
      "warehouse": "WH-MAIN",
      "policy_state": { "state": "active", "policy": "advisory" },
      "claims": [
        { "dispatch_line_uid": "DN-000017-L01", "dispatch_no": "DN-000017",
          "order_no": "SO-000042", "line_uid": "SO-000042-L01",
          "qty": 100, "policy": "advisory", "actor": "user:ul3byh",
          "taken_at": "2026-08-09T10:00:00.000Z" }
      ]
    }
  ]
}
```

Only **held** claims are returned. A released one is history, not a commitment.

<Warning>
**There is no field summing advisory and enforced claims, and you must not compute one.** The two mean different things — one is a note, the other is stock actually held — and adding them counts the same units twice under two different guarantees. The response has nowhere to put a total, deliberately.
</Warning>

An empty set under `none` and an empty set under `advisory` say different things, and the `policy_state` distinguishes them. Under `none` there is genuinely nothing to ask; that is not the same as "nothing is claimed".

## Force a claim open

```http
POST /claims/:dispatchLineUid/force-release     → claim.force_release
```

Supervisor, with a reason. For a claim left orphaned by a defect — stock held for something that no longer exists.

It releases the claim **and** its allocation, and says which. It records the operator's reason rather than the machine's, and it never un-ships anything: shipped units are left exactly where they are.

A second call reports that it released nothing and does not throw.

## How a claim stays honest

A claim is **derived**, not triggered. It is recomputed from the allocation entry on every write to the line's position, so:

- Reducing an allocation to zero **releases** the claim rather than leaving a zero-quantity row
- A short shipment leaves nothing claimed — the unshipped remainder is dropped, not held
- Deleting or cancelling a dispatch line leaves no claim behind
- A claim can never outlive the thing it was held for, because nothing has to remember to release it

The invariant is asserted after every act, not maintained by a list of triggers somebody has to keep complete.
