## Setup Checklist

<Steps>
  <Step title="Confirm account settings">
    Open **Settings > Account**. Check the account name, workspace URL, default timezone, and Slack connection if your automations send Slack messages.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create your organization structure">
    Add teams in the same hierarchy you use operationally. Teams are used for assignment, filtering, permissions, and reporting.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Define roles and permissions">
    Create roles such as Admin, Manager, Operator, Finance, or Vendor Coordinator. Give each role the minimum access it needs.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Invite users">
    Add users with their email, phone number, role, and teams. Use inactive status instead of deletion when you may need to keep history tied to a person.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Review tables and columns">
    Make sure each table has clear display fields, relation fields, required fields, status fields, and ownership fields.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create views for daily work">
    Build saved views such as "Open POs", "My pending dispatches", "At risk stock", or "Overdue work orders" so users do not need to rebuild filters.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add workflow actions">
    Use workflow buttons for business actions such as Confirm PO, Move to Production, Dispatch, Mark Received, Approve, Reject, or Close.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Automate repeatable work">
    Add automations for notifications, document generation, rollups, field updates, external API calls, and scheduled reminders.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Daily Operating Model

Most users spend their time in one of three places:

- **Home** for assigned queues, shortcuts, and high-priority work.
- **Tables** for searching, editing, importing, exporting, and reviewing records.
- **Reports** for management review and exception monitoring.

Administrators usually work in **Configurations** and **Settings** to adjust workflows, automations, document mappings, dashboard cards, users, roles, and account settings.

<Note>
If a user cannot see a table, view, column, or action button, check both their role permissions and the table or column-level permissions. ERPLite intentionally combines these controls so sensitive records can be shared narrowly.
</Note>

## AI Setup Loop

For every setup task, ask the AI to follow this loop:

1. **Choose the feature**: Explain why a table, view, workflow, automation, report, or permission is the right tool for the job.
2. **Configure one change**: Make the smallest meaningful change in ERPLite.
3. **Create or use a sample record**: Use realistic data that matches the operation.
4. **Verify the outcome**: Check what users can see, what actions are available, and what changed after the action runs.
5. **Document the decision**: Record the purpose, owner, expected behavior, and any limits.

<Tip>
Good AI setup prompts include the business goal, current pain point, example records, user roles, teams involved, and the exact proof you want after setup.
</Tip>
