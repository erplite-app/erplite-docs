Conditional field rules change how a field behaves based on the values of other fields. They make forms shorter, safer, and more business-aware.

## Rule Types

| Rule | What It Does | Example |
|------|--------------|---------|
| **Show when** | Shows the field only when a condition is true. | Show Rejection Reason only when Decision is Rejected. |
| **Lock when** | Makes the field read-only when a condition is true. | Lock Amount after Status is Approved. |
| **Require when** | Makes the field mandatory when a condition is true. | Require Dispatch Date when Status is Ready to Dispatch. |

Lock and required rules are enforced on every save, not just in the screen. This helps protect data even when records are changed through imports, automations, or AI-assisted operations.

## AI Guidance

### When to Use This Feature

An AI should use conditional fields when:

- A field is only relevant for certain statuses or categories.
- Users should not change a field after approval or confirmation.
- A field should be required only in specific scenarios.
- A form is too long because every possible field is always visible.

### How to Use It

<Steps>
  <Step title="Open the column settings">
    Edit the field that should become conditional.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Open Conditional Rules">
    Choose whether the field should be shown, locked, or required.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build the condition">
    Select the controlling field, operator, and value.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add an error message if enforced">
    For locked or required rules, provide a message that tells users what to fix.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Save and test">
    Try records that should pass and records that should fail.
  </Step>
</Steps>

### How to Verify

The AI should verify:

- The field appears only under the intended condition.
- The field locks when the lock condition is true.
- The field is required only when the required condition is true.
- Save is blocked with a useful message when required data is missing.
- Imports or automations cannot bypass enforced locked/required behavior.

## Best Practices

- Use conditional visibility to keep forms clean.
- Use locking for approved, confirmed, completed, or system-owned fields.
- Use conditional required rules for process-specific requirements.
- Keep error messages business-friendly and specific.
